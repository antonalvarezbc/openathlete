**Records** shows your best efforts for one sport at a time, the one you
practise most by default. Runs and rides never share a curve.

Each kind of record has its own chart and table:

| Record | Read along | Unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pace (running, walking...) or speed (cycling...) | distance, from 400 m to 100 km | the sport's pace or speed unit |
| Heart rate | duration, from 5 s to 2 h | bpm, best average |
| Power | duration, from 5 s to 2 h | watts, best average |
| Elevation gain | distance | metres climbed |

The table gives the date of each record and the activity it comes from:
select it to open the activity. The chart and the table compare all time
with the current season and the previous one (calendar years).

## How records are computed

Records come from the recorded stream of each activity, whatever the source:
connected devices or imported FIT, GPX and TCX files.

- Distance records need a GPS route. Each stretch ends exactly at the target
  distance, so they do not depend on how often the device recorded a point.
  Stops stay in the elapsed time: they make a stretch slower, never faster.
- Duration records weigh each sample by the time it covers. A gap of more than
  15 seconds between two samples counts as zero, so a dropout cannot hold a
  high value. Indoor sessions without GPS get power and heart rate records too.

A longer distance can occasionally show a faster pace than a shorter one: when
a stop splits a run, every short stretch may include it while the full
distance spreads it out.

When the computation improves, the records of existing activities are
recomputed in the background after the update: they can take a few minutes to
settle on a large history.
